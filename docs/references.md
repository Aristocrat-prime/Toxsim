# References and further reading

These sources are starting points for understanding pharmacokinetics and responsible model development. Check the publication details and current guidance before citing them in formal work.

1. **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** — Clinical Pharmacology resources and guidance documents. These explain how pharmacokinetic evidence is evaluated in drug development and regulatory review.  
   https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-resources/clinical-pharmacology

2. **National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI Bookshelf)** — Open biomedical textbooks and review material, including introductory pharmacology and pharmacokinetics chapters.  
   https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/

3. **Merck Manual Professional Edition** — Clinical reference material on poisoning, toxicology, and related medical topics. It is a reference source, not a replacement for local emergency or poison-centre guidance.  
   https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional

4. **World Health Organization (WHO)** — Publications and resources on chemical safety, public health, and poisoning prevention.  
   https://www.who.int/health-topics/chemical-safety

5. **International Council for Harmonisation (ICH)** — Guidelines relevant to nonclinical and clinical development, including pharmacokinetic and safety assessment.  
   https://www.ich.org/page/efficacy-guidelines

## Using sources responsibly

When adding a reference to this repository, include enough information for another person to find it: author or organisation, title, publication date or version where available, and a stable URL or DOI. Prefer primary research and official guidance for technical claims. A source that describes a parameter in one population should not be treated as proof that the same value applies to every population or exposure context.

This list is a starting point, not an exhaustive literature review. The repository has not yet established a verified, project-specific bibliography for any particular model implementation.
